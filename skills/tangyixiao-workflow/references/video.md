# Demonstration video mode

Use video when it helps people understand or use the actual project: an app,
website, plugin, or a substantial CLI workflow. Plan an initial public demonstration
and refresh it after meaningful experience changes. Do not require a promotional
film for every small script or redo it after every commit.

## Content and variants

Lead with the task the product helps complete, show one real operation end to end,
show the result and material limitations, then give a working install/use entry.
Use real application or terminal captures as the main evidence. Label illustrative
UI, accelerated footage, and edited skips; do not imply an unavailable application
was running or that fabricated account data are real results.

Default to an actual **MP4 deliverable at 60 fps** when the target/source supports
it. Check the exported frame rate and cadence; metadata saying 60 does not prove
the source operation was captured at 60. If source or platform limits require a
different frame rate, state the actual result and use the appropriate supported
export. Do not silently substitute a GIF or force 4K without a reason.

Keep an editable master. Recompose a 16:9 and a 9:16 variant when both are useful;
do not crop away essential controls, results, or Chinese captions. Prepare readable
subtitles, a clean video without platform watermarks, appropriate covers, and
consistent title/description/version/download information. Verify current official
Douyin, Bilibili, X, or other target platform specifications at production time;
do not hardcode old duration, codec, bitrate, caption, or upload limits.

Place a useful cover and playback link near the README's purpose/start section;
embed playback where supported and retain ordinary text instructions. Recheck
actual presentation on the target service. Producing an export does not authorize
posting it or messaging other people.

## Tools, rights, and disk

Use available recording/editing tools and any applicable production workflow;
this skill does not bundle a recorder, renderer, stock library, or uploader.
Confirm the brief within existing scope rather than introducing a new approval
round for routine edits already requested.

Use original or appropriately licensed footage, images, fonts, music, and voices;
preserve required attribution and brand conditions. Redact tokens, private paths,
accounts, and personal data from captures. Do not assume a public image is reusable.

Estimate source/proxy/frame-cache/export peak space before rendering. Reuse the
toolchain, bound renders and previews, and run one heavy media job at a time.
Retain useful master assets and final exports, then reconcile disposable frames,
caches, and rejected variants under the resource workflow. Do not copy the complete
media history into this toolkit or the receiving repository by default.
